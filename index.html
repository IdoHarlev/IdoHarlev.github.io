Cancer & spatial proteomics · Weizmann Institute

3D tissue reconstruction from multiplexed images

2023 – 2025 · KerenLab, with Dr. Leeat Keren, in collaboration with Dr. Shai Bagon

Multiplexed microscopy images tissue at single-cell resolution, but most analyses use 2D sections of 3D tissue. This work asks how much the sampling choice changes the biology you conclude, and how to recover 3D structure when dense 3D imaging is too costly.

  • Showed that one 2D section recovers global cell-type abundance reliably, while local statistics such as clustering and cell-cell interactions vary widely, especially for rare populations
  • Built a geometry-aware module that links cells across adjacent sections and estimates 3D cell centroids using cell-type-specific shape priors
  • Quantified the trade-off between section spacing, coverage and redundancy under a fixed imaging budget
  • Validated on a public imaging mass cytometry dataset and applied to an in-house CODEX dataset
  • Publication: I. Harlev, T. Oukhanov, R. Ben-Uri, L. Keren, S. Bagon. Sampling-Aware 3D Spatial Analysis in Multiplexed Imaging. CVPR 2026 Workshops (CVMI). arXiv · CVF
  • Award: Best Poster, Breaking New Methodologies in Biomedical Research, Weizmann Institute, July 2025

Materials science & biomechanics · Technion

Mechanics of soft matter and cardiomyocyte remodeling

2019 – 2021 · Soft Materials Lab, with Noy Cohen

Developed a thermodynamics-based model of how heart muscle cells remodel under mechanical and hormonal cues. The model links whole-cell deformation to sarcomere number, sarcomere stretch and myofibril count, with equations for protein production and degradation.

  • Compared four scenarios (isometric contraction and free radial dilation, each with unlimited or limited protein supply); boundary conditions strongly shaped the remodeling response, which may help explain how eccentric and concentric hypertrophy differ
  • Publication: I. Harlev, J. W. Holmes, N. Cohen. The influence of boundary conditions and protein availability on the remodeling of cardiomyocytes. Biomechanics and Modeling in Mechanobiology 21(1), 189–201, 2022. DOI
